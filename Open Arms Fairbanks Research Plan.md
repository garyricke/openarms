# **Comprehensive Institutional Profile and Longitudinal Analysis of Open Arms Child Development Center: A Case Study in Faith-Based Early Childhood Education and Organizational Resilience in Fairbanks, Alaska**

The landscape of early childhood education in the United States has undergone a profound transformation over the last two decades, moving from a primarily localized, informal sector to a highly regulated and professionally accredited industry. In the context of Fairbanks, Alaska, these national trends are amplified by unique geographic, economic, and logistical challenges. Open Arms Child Development Center (Open Arms CDC), established in 2000 as a non-profit ministry of Zion Lutheran Church, serves as a critical node in the regional "care economy," providing essential services to a community characterized by its extreme climate and specialized workforce.1 This report provides an exhaustive analysis of Open Arms CDC, examining its historical genesis, complex governance structures, pedagogical frameworks, fiscal health, and its prevailing reputation within the socio-political fabric of interior Alaska.

## **Historical Evolution and Foundational Identity**

The origin of Open Arms Child Development Center is fundamentally linked to the perceived needs of the Fairbanks community at the turn of the twenty-first century. Founded in 2000, the center was the result of a deliberate initiative by the Zion Lutheran Church to address a significant shortage of "safe, dependable, and loving" childcare options for working parents.1 In its foundational stages, the organization was framed not merely as a commercial daycare but as a "Child Development Ministry," a designation that continues to define its mission to "partner with families and nurture every child in a Christ-centered, developmentally appropriate environment".1 This dual identity—as both a religious ministry and a professional educational institution—has shaped its trajectory over the past twenty-five years.

The initial promise of the center was to foster the "whole child," targeting heart, mind, body, and spirit.1 This philosophy was rooted in Christian values but sought to integrate modern educational techniques, such as play-based learning and intentional teaching.1 Over time, the center expanded its reach, growing from a church-based initiative into a large-scale facility licensed to serve 236 children.5 This growth required the institution to navigate the increasingly complex requirements of national accreditation, eventually achieving certification from the National Association for the Education of Young Children (NAEYC), a distinction that signals a commitment to quality standards far exceeding basic state licensing requirements.4

### **Institutional Milestones and Regional Context**

The history of Open Arms CDC is also a history of civic engagement in Alaska. In 2009, the organization’s leadership played a visible role in state-level policy discussions. Lenetta Colbert, then the Director of Open Arms CDC and an adjunct professor at the University of Alaska Fairbanks, provided testimony before the House Education Standing Committee regarding House Bill 59\.6 Alongside her, Reverend Philip Kuehnert, the Pastor of Zion Lutheran and Chair of the Open Arms Institute Board of Directors, testified in support of the bill, which focused on the critical developmental benefits of funding pre-kindergarten programs.6 This involvement illustrates that from its early years, Open Arms CDC was positioned not just as a local provider but as a stakeholder in the broader educational advancement of the state.

## **Governance and Organizational Framework**

The governance of Open Arms Child Development Center is characterized by a multi-tiered oversight structure that reflects its status as a ministry of Zion Lutheran Church while maintaining its functional autonomy as a non-profit corporation.2 This arrangement ensures that the center’s operations are aligned with the theological values of the church while adhering to the fiscal and regulatory requirements of a 501(c)(3) organization.

This overlap ensures a seamless mission alignment between the religious parent organization and the educational ministry. ### **Physical and Administrative Infrastructure**

The center is physically located at 2980 Davis Road, a facility that is functionally and legally connected to Zion Lutheran Church.7 This physical integration allows for shared resources, such as the services of the Church Sexton, Robert Miranda, whose mess patrol ensures that the facilities remain clean and ready for both congregational use and daily childcare operations.7 Furthermore, the center’s administrative functions are bolstered by the church’s discipleship coordinator, Heather Koch, who oversees the educational aspects of the church, including youth activities and Sunday School, creating a holistic educational environment that bridges the gap between secular daycare and religious instruction.7

## **Pedagogical Framework and Programmatic Offerings**

The educational approach at Open Arms CDC is founded on a "whole child" philosophy that seeks to balance academic preparation with social-emotional development and spiritual growth.1 The curriculum is designed to be "developmentally appropriate," which in practice means that activities are tailored to the specific cognitive and physical stages of children from infancy through age twelve.1

### **Core Educational Pillars**

The center’s program is built around five central pillars:

1. **Academic Readiness:** The curriculum emphasizes literacy and early mathematics through hands-on, experiential learning activities.1  
2. **Social-Emotional Development:** Trained educators focus on fostering kindness, curiosity, and confidence, helping children navigate social interactions in a supportive environment.1  
3. **Spiritual Growth:** Daily routines include prayer, Bible stories, and songs, with weekly visits from the Zion Lutheran Pastor to reinforce Christian values.1  
4. **Physical Activity:** Daily playtime and structured physical activities are integrated into the schedule to promote motor skill development.1  
5. **Creative Expression:** Art, music, and imaginative play are used to encourage children to express their individuality and celebrate their unique gifts.1

### **Tiered Program Structure**

The center offers a comprehensive suite of programs categorized by age group, each maintaining specific teacher-to-child ratios to ensure safety and quality of instruction.

| Program Tier | Age Group | Curriculum Focus and Logistics |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Infant Care** | 6 Weeks to 1 Year | Focus on bonding, sensory stimulation, and basic developmental milestones in a nurturing environment.2 |
| **Wobblers** | 12 to 18 Months | Emphasizes mobility, initial social interaction, and language exploration.4 |
| **Toddlers** | 18 Months to 3 Years | Focuses on independence, vocabulary expansion, and social-emotional skills.4 |
| **Preschool** | 3 to 5 Years | Structured environment with a focus on pre-reading, math, science, and music; utilizes a 1:10 teacher-to-child ratio.8 |
| **Before & After School** | 5 to 12 Years | Provides homework support, breakfast, afternoon snacks, and transportation to/from local elementary schools.3 |
| **Summer Program** | School-Age | A full-day program for children up to 6th grade, featuring field trips and experiential learning during school holidays.2 |

The preschool program is particularly notable for its "learn by doing" philosophy, which uses field trips and special experiences to expand a child’s knowledge of the world.8 Academic subjects like science and mathematics are not taught in isolation but are woven into the natural learning activities of the day.8

## **Fiscal Sustainability and Financial Performance**

Open Arms Child Development Center operates as a significant financial entity within the Fairbanks non-profit sector. An analysis of its IRS Form 990 filings over the last several years reveals a complex financial narrative marked by substantial revenue shifts and a heavy reliance on program service fees, supplemented by critical grant funding.2

The center’s financial trajectory from 2021 to 2024 reflects the broader instability of the childcare industry during the post-pandemic recovery period.

The data reveals a stark decline in total revenue, which dropped by approximately 47.9% between 2021 and 2024\. This contraction is primarily driven by a significant reduction in program service revenue, which may be indicative of declining enrollment, staffing shortages that forced classroom closures, or a strategic reduction in capacity.10

The 2023 fiscal year was notable for a surge in grants and contributions, reaching $731,509, which allowed the center to post a healthy net income of $291,960 despite falling program revenue.2 A substantial portion of this funding likely came from organizations like Thread, which provided a grant of $163,599 for COVID-19 and program support.2 However, by 2024, these contributions normalized to $265,576, leading to a significant net loss of $225,019.10 This volatility underscores the center’s precarious financial position and its dependence on external subsidies to offset the high costs of operating a large, accredited facility in a high-cost region.

### **Enrollment Costs and Logistics**

Tuition serves as the lifeblood of the organization. For the 2025-2026 academic year, the center has maintained an annual registration fee of $70 per child or $130 per family.9 These fees are non-refundable and are required at the time of enrollment to secure a placement.9

While specific monthly tuition rates are subject to change and are typically provided to parents via the office, regional market data for Fairbanks indicates that private early childhood education costs often average between $6,600 and $6,900 per year, though full-time infant care in a high-quality, NAEYC-certified center can far exceed these figures.11 To mitigate these costs for both the center and families, Open Arms CDC participates in the USDA food program, which provides federal assistance for nutritious meals, ensuring that the school does not discriminate based on socio-economic status or race in the administration of its nutrition services.9

## **Human Capital and Workplace Dynamics**

The most significant operational challenge facing Open Arms CDC is the recruitment and retention of a skilled workforce. In 2023, the organization employed 62 individuals, representing a wide range of roles from lead teachers to administrative support staff.2 However, the human capital landscape is fraught with high turnover and reports of workplace stress.

### **Workforce Analysis and Compensation**

A critical data point in understanding the center's staffing issues is the compensation of its executive leadership. In 2023, the Executive Director received a salary of $37,188.2 For an organization managing a budget of nearly $2 million and a staff of 62, this compensation level is remarkably low, which may explain the frequent transitions in the director’s office.2

Employee reviews on platforms like Indeed and SimplyHired provide a window into the internal culture of the center. While the aggregate ratings for culture (3.7) and work-life balance (3.9) are relatively high, the ratings for management (3.3) and job security (3.1) highlight underlying friction.12

| Metric | Rating (out of 5.0) | Core Employee Concerns and Feedback |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Work-Life Balance** | 3.9 | Some flexibility is reported, but staff often face grueling hours (6:00 AM to 6:00 PM) to cover gaps.13 |
| **Management** | 3.3 | Feedback describes leadership as "uncompromising," "passive-aggressive," or "unprofessional," particularly during staff illnesses.13 |
| **Culture** | 3.7 | Employees value the Christian atmosphere and the children, though "petty drama" and "cliques" are occasionally mentioned.12 |
| **Pay & Benefits** | 3.3 | Frequently described as "underpaid" for the level of responsibility and stress involved in childcare.12 |
| **Job Security** | 3.1 | High turnover leads to a "hectic" environment where staff are moved between classrooms without notice.14 |

Staff members have reported that the center is a "great starter job" for those new to early childhood education.12 However, the "high turnover rate" means that veteran teachers are often overworked, and classroom support is frequently insufficient.13 Specific complaints from 2024 highlight a management style that allegedly "gaslights" employees regarding their health or refuses to compromise on scheduling even with medical documentation.14 One review went so far as to describe management as "desperate to keep anyone," suggesting that the labor shortage in Fairbanks has forced the center to relax some of its standards for hiring and retention.15

### **The Impact of turnover on Quality**

The instability of the workforce has direct implications for the quality of care. Parents and staff have both observed that the frequent change in personnel can lead to "intentional misrepresentation" and "negligence".16 When staff are constantly quitting and being rehired, the continuity of care that is essential for a child’s social-emotional development is broken. Furthermore, the practice of "pushing" teachers from their assigned rooms to cover other areas "temporarily" creates a sense of chaos for both the educators and the students.13

## **Community Standing and Public Perception**

In the Fairbanks North Star Borough, Open Arms CDC is a well-known institution, but its reputation is increasingly complex. While it is praised for its security and its mission, it has also become a focal point for community debate regarding the quality of licensed childcare.

### **Parental Feedback and Safety Concerns**

External reviews from parents present a dichotomy. On Winnie and other professional portals, the center is recognized for its licensed status and its comprehensive facilities.5 However, on community forums like Reddit and Facebook, parents have expressed significant alarm regarding safety and hygiene.16

In 2022 and 2023, discussions on the "r/Fairbanks" subreddit highlighted several recurring issues:

* **Hygiene and Negligence:** Parents reported that toddlers frequently returned home "disgustingly dirty" with unexplained scratches and bruises, which they attributed to a lack of adequate supervision.16  
* **Safety Incidents:** Observations of toddlers standing on tables and children walking out of classrooms unattended were cited as evidence of a "great big scary mess".16  
* **Health Transparency:** Allegations surfaced that the center failed to properly notify parents of health outbreaks, such as pink eye, and instead removed notices from walls to keep the information "secret".16  
* **Legal Scrutiny:** Mention of "crazy lawsuits" circulated on Facebook group pages, creating a perception of institutional risk, although the specific details of these cases are not fully elucidated in the provided snippets.16

Despite these criticisms, the center remains a popular choice for many families who value the convenience of the breakfast program, the secure keypad entry system, and the Christian framework.16 The persistent waiting list suggests that the demand for childcare in Fairbanks is so acute that families may feel they have little choice but to utilize a large, licensed facility despite its reported flaws.9

## **Regulatory Compliance and Professional Accreditation**

One of the center’s primary defense mechanisms against the current crisis in childcare is its commitment to high-level accreditation. Open Arms CDC is not only licensed by the State of Alaska (License \#11975) but is also certified by the National Association for the Education of Young Children (NAEYC).4

### **The Significance of NAEYC Certification**

NAEYC accreditation is a voluntary process that requires centers to meet ten research-based standards of quality. For Open Arms CDC, this certification serves several critical functions:

* **Quality Benchmark:** It provides a framework for teacher training and curriculum development that goes beyond state minimums.4  
* **Funding Eligibility:** Certain grants and contributions, such as those from Thread, are often contingent upon a center’s accreditation status or their participation in "Learn & Grow," Alaska’s Quality Recognition and Improvement System.2  
* **Market Differentiation:** In a market where parents are increasingly wary of "negligence," the NAEYC seal acts as a proxy for trust, even when individual reviews are negative.4

However, maintaining this certification is an arduous task in an environment of high turnover. NAEYC standards require lead teachers to have specific qualifications (e.g., Child Development Associate credentials or degrees in Early Childhood Education), which are difficult to maintain when staff are "quitting and coming back" with regularity.16

## **Strategic Challenges and Future Outlook**

As Open Arms Child Development Center enters its twenty-sixth year of operation, it stands at a crossroads. The transition to interim leadership, combined with a significant fiscal deficit in 2024, indicates that the current model may be reaching a point of exhaustion.7

### **Economic and Demographic Headwinds**

The center’s future will be heavily influenced by the broader economic conditions of the Fairbanks North Star Borough. The high cost of heating, insurance, and labor in the Alaskan interior creates a high "break-even" point for any childcare facility. The 2024 revenue drop suggests that the center may be struggling to keep its classrooms full, perhaps due to the labor shortage or a shift in parental preference toward smaller, home-based settings.10

Furthermore, the center must contend with the "childcare cliff"—the expiration of federal pandemic-era stabilization funds that buoyed the industry for several years. The 80% change in grants observed in 2023 was a lifeline that may not be repeatable in the current political and economic climate.2

### **Recommendations for Organizational Resilience**

To navigate the coming years, Open Arms CDC would likely need to focus on several strategic areas:

1. **Leadership Stabilization:** Finding a permanent Director who can address the systemic cultural issues cited by staff will be essential to stopping the "high turnover rate".7  
2. **Compensation Reform:** The gap between the Executive Director’s salary and the responsibilities of the role suggests a need for a re-evaluation of the compensation structure to attract and retain professional talent.2  
3. **Community Transparency:** Actively addressing the concerns raised on social media and in the Reddit community regarding safety and health notices could help repair the center’s reputation among local parents.16  
4. **Grant Diversification:** Given the volatility of program revenue, the board must continue to seek diverse funding streams, potentially leveraging its relationship with the Zion Lutheran congregation for more direct support or endowment building.2

## **Synthesis of Institutional Value**

Despite the challenges outlined in this report, Open Arms Child Development Center remains an indispensable part of the Fairbanks infrastructure. For over two decades, it has provided a "safe, dependable, and loving place" for thousands of children to grow.1 Its identity as a ministry of Zion Lutheran Church provides it with a level of facility stability and community backing that many secular, for-profit centers lack.7

The organization’s ability to "grow hearts, minds, and faith" is not just a marketing slogan; it is a foundational philosophy that has survived numerous changes in leadership and economic cycles.1 However, the data indicates that the "ministry" model is currently under strain from the "market" reality of the labor crisis and post-pandemic economic shifts. The center’s success in its next quarter-century will depend on its ability to reconcile these two identities—ensuring that it remains a Christ-centered ministry while evolving into a workplace that can sustain a professional, long-term workforce.1

In conclusion, Open Arms CDC is a microcosm of the wider American childcare dilemma. It is an organization with a noble mission and a solid physical foundation that is currently battling the systemic issues of low wages, high turnover, and fluctuating revenue. Its journey reflects the resilience of faith-based organizations and the enduring need for high-quality early childhood education in one of the most challenging environments in North America.1
